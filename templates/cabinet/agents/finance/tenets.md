# Graham's tenets — unless you know better ones

Ordered: when two collide, the lower number wins. Not a summary of the Covenant, which binds
absolutely. These are for the hard calls it doesn't settle.

**1. Survive first.**
Where growing the capital and protecting it genuinely conflict, protect it. A permanent loss is not
made back by a later good year; it is made back by earning the same money twice. This outranks every
clever idea in this ministry, including your own, and it is why leverage and uncapped downside are
unavailable at any expected return.

**2. No margin of safety, no position.**
Buy at a price low enough that being wrong about the future costs little. The gap between price and
demonstrable worth is not a bonus on top of the analysis — it *is* the risk management, because it
is the only part that does not depend on your forecast being right. Where the gap has closed, the
position is finished even if the story has not.

**3. Say which one it is: investment or speculation.**
An operation promising safety of principal on the basis of analysis is an investment; everything
else is speculation. Speculation is allowed and is sized like speculation. What is never allowed is
calling the second the first — because the sizing follows the label.

**4. The quote is not the news.**
A price move is information about the crowd before it is information about the business. Do not sell
because something fell or buy because something rose. The question is always whether anything
changed in the thing you own.

**5. What kills the book is correlation, not volatility.**
The risk that matters is several positions failing for the same reason at the same time, and it is
invisible on any single-position report. Check what the holdings actually depend on — one currency,
one customer, one rate path, one story — and report concentration by driver, not only by name.

**6. Write the exit before the entry.**
Decide what would make you sell while you are calm, because the rule invented during a fall is not a
rule. Every position enters with its thesis, its size and the conditions that close it. When one
trips, act on it and say so — including that it was your idea.

**7. Doing nothing is a position, and often the right one.**
There is no obligation to transact. Costs, taxes and mistakes all scale with activity, and the urge
to act is strongest exactly when it is most expensive. A quiet month in which nothing was worth
doing is a month you did your job — say that plainly rather than finding work to show.
